Method and verification

How every value was checked, what was excluded, and what the study cannot tell you.

The rule for this project was simple and strict: a number either comes from an official page, or it does not appear.

1. What counts as an official source

  • The bank's own domain — the product page, rate sheet, fee schedule or disclosure PDF on the bank's website.
  • The FDIC — deposit insurance rules, the national rate table, and the BankFind institution record (via api.fdic.gov).
  • The U.S. Treasury — yields, as republished in the FDIC national rate table.

Aggregators, review sites, affiliate "best rate" lists, news articles and the comparison tables inside banks' own marketing pages were not used as value sources. For example, SoFi's page compares itself to Chase at 0.02% and Bank of America at 0.04%; those are third-party figures SoFi attributes to a data vendor, so they do not appear as Chase or BofA values here.

2. How each row was verified

  1. Identify the legal entity and FDIC certificate through the FDIC BankFind API, and check that the institution is active.
  2. Open the bank's own product page and record: APY (with the page's own effective date), minimum to open, minimum to earn the APY, monthly fee and waiver, withdrawal rules, app, security controls, insurance statement, bonus and opening requirements.
  3. Paste the exact wording into the notes where the wording is doing the work — for example "no minimum balance required to open an account or earn APY".
  4. If the page will not produce the value (ZIP gate, login wall, JavaScript-only table), record the reason and leave it blank.
  5. Store the source URL with the value so any reader can re-run the check.

3. How the return figures are calculated

Interest = principal × ((1 + APY / 100)years − 1). Because an APY already embeds compounding, it is used directly as an annual factor. No fees are deducted (all the high-rate accounts are fee-free), no taxes are modelled, and no rate changes are assumed. Projections start on 2026-10-22, the account-opening date used throughout.

Blended APYs in the FDIC section are deposit-weighted averages of the published APYs applied to each slice. Promotion maths assumes the boosted rate applies for exactly the stated promotional period and the standard rate applies afterwards.

4. What this study cannot tell you

  • How a rate will move. Every account here is variable-rate. Past rate behaviour is not published in a form that could be verified, so no trend is claimed.
  • Your actual APY at a ZIP-gated bank. For Chase, Bank of America, Citi and TD it is location- and relationship-dependent. What is published is the structure of the fee, not a single number.
  • Which account you will be approved for. All of these banks may decline an application; SoFi and Capital One say so explicitly.
  • Tax. Interest is taxable income. No after-tax figures are shown.

5. Refresh instructions

All values live in data/banks.json. Re-checking means opening the sources URL for a product, updating the value and apy_as_of, then re-running python3 build.py, which regenerates every page and rebuilds data/analysis.json. The build refuses to render a verified APY that has no tier value or no source link.

6. Limitations and conflicts

  • No affiliate links, no referral codes, and no compensation relationship with any bank.
  • The "big national bank" filter uses FDIC total assets from the institution file; it is a size screen, not a quality judgement.
  • Figures were read on 2026-10-22 in a U.S. timezone; a page edited later the same day may differ.
