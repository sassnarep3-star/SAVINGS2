What you generally need to open an account online

  • U.S. residential address, Social Security number or Tax ID, date of birth, and a government-issued ID (stated on Synchrony’s “get started in minutes” section: personal information including SSN/Tax ID helps verify the application) — banks run identity verification (CIP) under the USA PATRIOT Act.
  • An external bank account and routing number for the initial funding transfer. The online opening limit matters at scale: Bank of America caps online savings openings at $250,000; Marcus allows up to $1,000,000 per Online Savings Account ($3M per owner across accounts); PNC reserves the right to refuse deposits that push combined savings/money-market balances above $5,000,000.
  • Several promos require the account be opened through a specific offer page or with a code (Capital One BONUS1500; Chase offer enrollment; PNC bonus page; U.S. Bank campus pages) — opening from a generic page can forfeit the offer.
  • Joint ownership: most of these accounts allow a co-owner (SoFi offers joint banking; PNC/Chase/branch banks support joint titling). Joint titling changes FDIC coverage — see FDIC & security.

Withdrawal limits context: federal Regulation D transfer limits on savings accounts were lifted in 2020, but banks can still set their own rules. Truist charges $5 per withdrawal beyond six per cycle; Ally caps at 10 per cycle (no fee); the other banks reviewed publish no savings withdrawal-limit fee on the pages reviewed. ⚠ Withdrawal-limit fees for Chase/BofA/Wells/Citi/PNC savings are documented in each bank’s full fee schedule rather than the pages captured here — confirm on the linked fee pages before relying on unlimited withdrawals.
