FDIC insurance, security and mobile apps

Every bank on this site is an FDIC member — verified on October 22, 2026 against the FDIC’s official institutions API (records linked in the table below). Rules summarized from fdic.gov.

The FDIC rules that matter for savings

  • $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, for each account ownership category. The standard insurance amount has been $250,000 per depositor since 2008 and is permanent law. Principal and interest are insured together.
  • Coverage is automatic when you open a deposit account at an FDIC-insured bank — no application, no cost to you.
  • “Per bank” means per charter: deposits at two different FDIC-insured banks are insured separately. (One bank with two brands is one charter — this now matters for Capital One, which absorbed Discover Bank.)
  • Categories stack: single accounts ($250,000 per owner per bank), joint accounts ($250,000 per co-owner per bank, across all joint accounts at that bank), certain retirement accounts (e.g., IRA CDs — a separate $250,000 per owner per bank), and revocable trust accounts ($250,000 per owner for each unique eligible beneficiary named on the trust, up to five beneficiaries = up to $1,250,000 per owner).
  • Verify your own coverage with the FDIC’s official EDIE calculator (Electronic Deposit Insurance Estimator).
  • What is not insured: investments (stocks, bonds, mutual funds, crypto), safe deposit box contents, and annuities/insurance products, even when sold by a bank.
  • If a bank fails: the FDIC as receiver either moves deposits to another bank or pays depositors directly, insured amounts typically within a few business days.

Insuring more than $250,000 (the user’s stated goal — maximum FDIC coverage): (1) split deposits across separately chartered FDIC banks — e.g., $250,000 at Synchrony (3.55%) + $250,000 at Marcus (3.50%) + $250,000 at Capital One (3.20%) keeps everything insured at strong rates; (2) use ownership categories at the same bank — a couple with a $500,000 joint account plus $250,000 each in single accounts has up to $1,000,000 insured at one bank; (3) revocable trusts multiply coverage per qualifying beneficiary; (4) SoFi advertises FDIC coverage up to $3M via its Insured Deposit Program, which sweeps balances beyond $250,000 to a network of participating FDIC banks — coverage then depends on those partner banks. Confirm any structure with the EDIE calculator before depositing.
