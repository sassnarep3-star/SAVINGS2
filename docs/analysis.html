How competitive are these rates over the life of the deposit?

Savings APYs are variable: they can change the day after you open the account. This page assesses what drives each bank’s rate and what that means for a deposit held for years, using officially verified data only (federalreserve.gov, fdic.gov, and bank pages).

The rate environment, from official sources

  • Federal funds target range: 3.75%–4.00%. On September 16, 2026, the FOMC raised the target range by 25 basis points to 3¾–4 percent — its first increase since 2023, responding to elevated inflation (PCE ~3.7% projected for 2026). FOMC statement
  • More tightening is projected, not guaranteed. The median FOMC participant projection at the September meeting put the funds rate at ~4.1% at the end of 2026 and through 2027, implying at most one more hike this year; the next meeting is October 27–28, 2026. Projections are not commitments. Press conference transcript (PDF)
  • FDIC national average savings rate: 0.38% (weighted average across all insured institutions; $2,500 product tier), as of October 19, 2026. National average money market: 0.63%; 12-month CD: 1.76%. fdic.gov national rates
  • Dispersion is extreme: the highest verified big-bank standard savings APY (Synchrony, 3.55%) is ~89× the FDIC national average and 355× Chase/Truist’s 0.01%. The average is dragged down by large branch banks’ near-zero rates — the “national average” is not a benchmark for what a large bank pays, but a floor.

Which rates move, and which don’t

Rate type Examples (verified) How it behaves over the deposit’s life Evidence
Online-bank variable APY Synchrony 3.55% · Marcus 3.50% · CapOne 3.20% · Amex 3.20% · Ally 3.10% Tracks the general level of short-term rates with a spread below the fed funds range (currently ~0.2–0.9pt depending on the bank). When the Fed moves, these rates tend to move — e.g., SoFi’s published new-member boost rose from 0.90% APY (terms “as of 9/23/26”) to 1.10% (“as of 10/20/26”) in the weeks after the hike. With the Fed’s median projection at 4.1% end-2026, these APYs are more likely to hold or rise than fall in the near term — but a future easing cycle would pull them down. FDIC averages · FOMC
Brick-and-mortar standard APY Chase 0.01% · Truist 0.01% · Wells 0.01% · Citi 0.03% · BofA 0.04% · US Bank 0.05% Effectively anchored near zero regardless of Fed policy — these banks fund deposits through branches and don’t compete on savings yield. In a 3.75–4.00% fed funds world they still pay 0.01–0.05%; there is no realistic rate scenario in which they become competitive. Chase rate sheet · vs. FDIC avg 0.38%
Relationship rates US Bank Smartly 3.50% (checking + $25k CQB) · Wells Premier 0.05% · Chase Premier 0.02% Designed to attract and keep a full banking relationship. Competitive only where the bank chooses to compete (U.S. Bank’s Smartly is a genuine exception at 3.50%). They persist as long as conditions are met, but they are variable and can be repriced. usbank.com
Promotional rates SoFi +1.10% × 6mo · Citi 3.75% × 3mo · Wells 3.25% new customers · Marcus referral +1.00% × 3mo Time-boxed by definition. They improve first-year returns and then revert to the standard variable APY. Citi’s promo ended 10/22/2026 (feature discontinued 10/23/2026). Expect new promos each quarter — banks rotate them; chasing promos costs a credit inquiry and account churn. sofi.com · citi.com

What this means for a deposit held for years

  • The bank you choose matters far more than the rate cycle. The gap between Synchrony at 3.55% and Chase at 0.01% is 3.54 percentage points. The entire plausible band of Fed moves over the next year (±0.5pt) is an order of magnitude smaller than the gap between the top online banks and the big branch banks. Choosing the right type of bank dominates every other decision.
  • Within the top tier, differences are small and fluid. The six online-first national banks sit within a 0.45pt band (3.10%–3.55%). Rankings swap with rate changes; today’s #1 (Synchrony) may not be #1 next quarter. Bonuses (e.g., Capital One’s $1,500) can outweigh a 0.35pt rate difference in year one on a $250,000 balance.
  • Watch two dates if you open now: the FOMC meeting on October 27–28, 2026 (a hike would pressure savings rates up), and each bank’s right to change variable APYs at any time. One-year CDs are an alternative for locking rates (FDIC national avg 12-mo CD 1.76%; Capital One 360 CDs advertise up to 4.40% APY) — outside this study’s savings scope, but relevant if rates later fall.
  • Promotional rates are worth taking only if the underlying rate is competitive. SoFi (3.30% base + 1.10% boost + $400) and Capital One (3.20% + up to $1,500) both have competitive bases; Chase’s $200 bonus on a 0.01% account still leaves you ~$8,500/yr behind at $250,000.
  • Brick-and-mortar still makes sense for: in-person service, cashier’s checks, same-day large cash needs, or if you value one relationship across checking/savings/credit. But as a pure yield vehicle, their standard savings products are not competitive — and fee traps (BofA $8/mo; Chase/Wells $5/mo) can make them negative-return.

≤ $250,000 — simplest Synchrony 3.55% (no conditions) or Marcus 3.50% Highest ongoing APYs with zero requirements, zero fees, zero minimums; both are large FDIC-insured national banks with 4.6+ rated apps. Fully FDIC-insured at $250,000 in a single account.
≤ $250,000 — maximize year one SoFi (~$10,025 first year: 3.30% + 6-mo 4.40% boost + $400 bonus, with direct deposit) or Capital One ($9,500 first year: 3.20% + $1,500 BONUS1500) Verified offers add $1,200–$1,900 of first-year value over the base rate. After year one both pay competitive standard rates (3.30% / 3.20%).
≤ $250,000 — want a branch bank U.S. Bank Smartly Savings 3.50% (with Bank Smartly Checking + $25,000 CQB) or Wells Fargo Platinum 3.25% (new savings customers, $25,000+) The only branch banks paying near-online rates. U.S. Bank matches Marcus at 3.50% ongoing while conditions hold; Wells’ promo applies only to new savings customers and is variable.
$250,000–$500,000 Split across two separate FDIC charters — e.g., $250,000 at Synchrony + $250,000 at Marcus (or Capital One/Amex/Ally/SoFi) $250,000 insured per bank per ownership category. Splitting across the top-rate banks costs almost nothing in yield (3.10%–3.55% at every one of them).
$500,000+ Three-plus banks at $250,000 each, or joint/trust ownership categories at the same bank, or SoFi’s Insured Deposit Program (up to $3M advertised) Joint accounts add $250,000 per co-owner; revocable trusts add $250,000 per owner per unique beneficiary (up to 5). Verify any structure with the FDIC’s EDIE calculator.

Caveats. (1) All APYs shown are variable and were verified 10/16–10/22/2026; the banks can reprice at any time. (2) Promotional terms can be withdrawn or changed — the linked offer pages are authoritative. (3) Projected interest ignores taxes (interest and bonuses are taxable income) and ignores the opportunity cost of chasing bonuses instead of rates. (4) This is research, not financial advice.
