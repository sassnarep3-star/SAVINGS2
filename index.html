The answer, in one paragraph

On 2026-10-22 the highest fully verified savings APY among the 22 active accounts screened is 3.75% (CIT Bank Platinum Savings). The two branch banks that publish a rate publicly (Truist and Wells Fargo) pay 0.01%, and Chase, Bank of America, Citi, TD, Capital One, Citizens and Fifth Third publish no rate a public page can capture at all — those rates are ZIP-, region- or relationship-gated, so they are left blank here instead of being guessed. A $250,000 deposit therefore earns about $9,375.00 in year one at the best verified rate, versus about $25.00 at 0.01% and $950.00 at the FDIC national average.

What this project found

  1. The rate spread is enormous. 3.75% vs 0.38% at the average bank is a 9.9× difference in interest for identical FDIC protection. On $250,000 that is $8,425.00 of extra interest in one year.
  2. Branch-bank brand costs you the interest. The only branch banks that publish a rate publicly (Truist 0.01%, Wells Fargo 0.01%) sit at the bottom of the table, and the four largest banks publish no retrievable rate at all; the highest verified rates come from branch-free U.S. banks (CIT/First Citizens, Barclays Bank Delaware, Goldman Sachs/Marcus, SoFi, American Express, Ally).
  3. "Max FDIC insurance" is a structuring question, not a bank question. Coverage is $250,000 per depositor, per ownership category, per bank. $1,000,000 in one name needs four banks; a joint account needs two; a trust naming five beneficiaries is covered to $1,250,000 at one bank. See FDIC coverage.
  4. Savings rates do not beat the Treasury curve today. The 12-month Treasury yielded 4.54% and the 5-year 5.09% (FDIC table, as of 2026-10-19), versus 3.75% for the best verified savings account. A savings account buys liquidity, not yield — the competitiveness page quantifies the trade-off.
  5. Only 16 of 22 active accounts could be verified end-to-end. The rest are on the Flagged for Review page with the exact reason (ZIP gate, JavaScript-only rate table, or no published rate).
