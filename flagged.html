Flagged for review

Everything that could not be verified, and every irregularity found while checking. Nothing here is hidden in a footnote.

This page exists so that nothing on this site is a guess. Every value that could not be confirmed from an official public page is listed here with the reason, and the value is left blank in the tables.

7 of 23 active products have no fully verifiable APY. Seven publish nothing a public page will disclose, PNC's figure exists only in a page title, and one further product (Discover) is included solely to record that its charter is now inactive. Not because the banks are hiding anything illegal — because the biggest banks publish rates only after a ZIP code or login, or inject them with JavaScript that a static fetch cannot read. Substituting an aggregator's number would have made the table look complete and would have been unverifiable.

4. Specific items I want a human to review

  1. PNC 3.15%. The number appears in PNC's own page title, but the page body says the product is not available in my area, and there is no effective date. It is used in the return tables with a flag. If PNC's product is not available in your region, ignore the row.
  2. Capital One's APY renders as "NaN". The page text says the APY is "effective 10/21/2026", then prints "NaN APY". The value is almost certainly injected client-side. Left blank.
  3. Bank of America's $XXXX placeholders. The public page shows literal "$XXXX" for the monthly fee and the fee-waiver balance. That is a rendering fault on BofA's side, not a missing value in my research.
  4. CIT's rate sheet is dated 2026-07-01 — nearly four months older than every other verified quote (2026-10-07 to 2026-10-21). If CIT repriced since then, the 3.75% in the return tables is stale. Re-check at application.
  5. CIT trust accounts. The FDIC structure for $1.25M at one bank assumes a trust-titled account. I verified that Ally offers trust-titled savings; I did not verify that CIT does. The FDIC page presents that structure as the highest single-bank capacity available, not as a recommendation for CIT specifically.
  6. SoFi's $3,000,000 FDIC claim comes from SoFi's marketing copy about a partner-bank network. The FDIC's rule is $250,000 per depositor per bank. Treat the $3M figure as a claim to verify with SoFi and EDIE, not as an established fact.
  7. Discover is not a separate bank any more. FDIC institution data lists Discover Bank (cert 5649) as inactive, and Capital One says Capital One and Discover accounts opened from 2025-05-18 are aggregated for insurance. Anyone using Discover as a "second bank" for extra coverage is likely doubling up on one $250,000 limit.
  8. Two banks with no published rate date (Wells Fargo, HSBC) and one with a partial-source rate (PNC). The rates are quoted as shown, but they cannot be tied to a specific day.

5. What was checked and passed

  • FDIC charter status and certificate number for every bank, via the FDIC BankFind API (data index dated 2026-10-16).
  • Every APY, minimum, fee and bonus against the bank's own page on 2026-10-22, with the page's own "as of" date where published.
  • FDIC limits against the FDIC's Deposit Insurance, Understanding Deposit Insurance and Your Insured Deposits pages.
  • National average rate and Treasury yields against the FDIC National Rates and Rate Caps table (October 2026, revised 2026-10-19).
