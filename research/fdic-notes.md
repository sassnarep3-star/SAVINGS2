# FDIC Insurance — Verified Notes

_Read from fdic.gov on 2026-10-22._

## Core rule

"The standard deposit insurance amount is **$250,000 per depositor, per FDIC-insured
bank, per ownership category**." Coverage is automatic for deposit accounts opened at
an FDIC-insured bank; insurance is calculated dollar-for-dollar, principal plus accrued
interest through the date of default.
Source: https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/faq

## Ownership categories relevant to maximizing coverage at ONE bank

Source: https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/financial-products-insured

| Ownership category | FDIC-stated coverage limit |
|---|---|
| Single account (one owner, no beneficiaries) | All single accounts of the same person at the same bank added together, insured up to $250,000 |
| Joint account | Each co-owner's share of all joint accounts at the same bank insured up to $250,000 (so a 2-owner joint account can be insured up to $500,000 total) |
| Trust account (incl. POD/payable-on-death beneficiary designations) | # of owners × # of beneficiaries × $250,000, not to exceed $1,250,000 per owner for all trust accounts at the bank |
| Certain retirement accounts (e.g., IRA deposits) | All such accounts of the same person at the same bank added together, insured up to $250,000 |

Practical implications for the goal "max FDIC insurance for my savings":

- One person, savings only, one bank, no beneficiaries named → max fully-insured = $250,000.
- Naming POD beneficiaries (trust category) raises coverage at the same bank:
  1 owner × 5 beneficiaries × $250,000 = $1,250,000 (the per-owner trust cap).
- A joint account with 2 co-owners → up to $500,000; joint + beneficiaries moves the
  account into the trust category per FDIC rules.
- Spreading deposits across multiple separately-chartered FDIC-insured banks multiplies
  the $250,000 single-account coverage (each bank = separate limit).
- FDIC's official calculator: EDIE, https://edie.fdic.gov/
- Check a bank's insured status: FDIC BankFind, https://banks.data.fdic.gov/bankfind-suite/bankfind

## Products covered / not covered

FDIC covers deposit products: checking, savings, money market deposit accounts, CDs.
Investment products (mutual funds, stocks, bonds, annuities) are NOT covered even if
bought at a bank. (Same FAQ source as above.)
