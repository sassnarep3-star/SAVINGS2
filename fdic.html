FDIC coverage

How the $250,000 limit works, how many banks a large deposit needs, and the structures that keep 100% of it insured.

"Maximum FDIC insurance" is not a property of a bank — it is a property of how the account is titled. The FDIC covers $250,000 per depositor, per FDIC-insured bank, for each account ownership category. Every account on this site is at an FDIC-insured institution; the practical question is how much you can hold at each one.

The FDIC's own warning matters here: "All of your deposits in the same ownership category in the same FDIC-insured bank are added together for the purpose of determining FDIC deposit insurance coverage." Two savings accounts at the same bank in your name are one $250,000 limit, not two.

4. Traps that silently break coverage

  • Two brands, one charter. CIT Bank is a division of First Citizens Bank, so CIT deposits combine with First Citizens deposits for coverage. Capital One states that Capital One and Discover accounts opened on or after 2025-05-18 are counted together — the FDIC record shows Discover Bank (cert 5649) as inactive. Two familiar brands, one $250,000 limit.
  • Fintech "network" insurance is not the same as a bank deposit. SoFi advertises coverage up to $3,000,000 through a network of participating banks; that is the bank's own marketing statement, not an FDIC rule, and it depends on how the sweep partners are structured. Verify with the bank and with EDIE before relying on it.
  • Trust coverage requires a real trust account. The $1,250,000 limit applies to accounts titled in the name of a valid revocable or irrevocable trust naming five or more eligible beneficiaries. Of the high-rate banks checked here, only Ally states on its savings page that the account can be opened in the name of a trust.
  • Interest counts toward the limit. Coverage is principal plus accrued interest through the date of failure, so a $250,000 deposit sitting at exactly the cap can drift over it as interest accrues.

5. Check it yourself

The FDIC's own calculator (EDIE) is the authoritative check for a specific titling: edie.fdic.gov. Bank charter status is verified through FDIC BankFind. Every FDIC certificate number on the accounts page links to that bank's FDIC record.
